#Part 1: Introduction to Software Engineering

Explain what software engineering is and discuss its importance in the technology industry.
Software engineering is the application of engineering principles to the design, development, testing, deployment, and maintenance of software systems. 
Software engineering automates repetitive tasks, boosting productivity.
Well-designed software helps companies scale operations. E-commerce platforms like Amazon use data-driven algorithms to recommend products, enhancing customer experience and sales.

Identify and describe at least three key milestones in the evolution of software engineering.
The Birth of Software Engineering (1968) – The term software engineering was formally introduced at the NATO Software Engineering Conference to address the "software crisis." As computers became more powerful, early software development faced issues like cost overruns, inefficiencies, and system failures. This led to the development of structured programming, focusing on modular and organized code.
The Rise of Object-Oriented Programming (OOP) (1980s–1990s) – The introduction of OOP languages like C++, Java, and Python revolutionized software development by promoting code reusability, encapsulation, and modularity. This shift made software more scalable, maintainable, and efficient, significantly influencing modern software design patterns.
The Agile and Devops Revolution (2000s–Present) – Traditional software development models, like Waterfall, were slow and rigid. The introduction of Agile methodologies (Scrum, Kanban) and DevOps transformed the industry by enabling faster, iterative development, continuous integration, and real-time deployment. Companies like Google, Amazon, and Microsoft leverage DevOps to accelerate software delivery and improve reliability.

List and briefly explain the phases of the Software Development Life Cycle.
Planning – Identifying project goals, feasibility, and resource requirements. Teams define scope, budget, and timelines to ensure a structured development process.
Requirement Analysis – Gathering and documenting user needs and system specifications. This phase ensures the final product aligns with business and customer expectations.
Design – Creating system architecture, interface layouts, and database structures. Developers and designers outline how the software will function and interact with users.
Implementation (Coding) – Writing the actual code based on design specifications. Developers use programming languages and frameworks to build the software.
Testing – Identifying and fixing bugs through unit, integration, and system testing. This ensures the software meets quality standards and functions correctly.
Deployment – Releasing the software for end users. It can be launched in stages (beta testing) or fully deployed based on project requirements.
Maintenance & Support – Updating, optimizing, and fixing issues post-deployment. This phase ensures long-term performance, security, and scalability.

Compare and contrast the Waterfall and Agile methodologies. Provide examples of scenarios where each would be appropriate.
The Waterfall methodology follows a sequential and linear approach where each phase is completed before moving to the next while agile is iterative and flexible, allowing for continuous improvements and modifications throughout the development process.
Waterfall consists of fixed phases, including planning, design, implementation, testing, deployment, and maintenance, which are executed in order, while agile follows a cyclical approach, where work is divided into small iterations or sprints, incorporating continuous development and feedback.
In Waterfall, requirements are defined upfront and rarely change during development while agile, however, accommodates evolving requirements, allowing for adjustments based on customer needs and market demands.
Waterfall has limited customer involvement after the initial planning phase, as feedback is usually incorporated at the end, while agile encourages high customer involvement with frequent reviews, feedback loops, and collaboration throughout the project.
Waterfall delivers the entire product at the end of the development cycle while agile, on the other hand, delivers small, functional increments in shorter time frames, ensuring faster time-to-market.
Waterfall is rigid and difficult to modify once a phase is completed, whereas Agile is highly adaptable, allowing for changes even in later stages of development.
Testing in Waterfall is performed after development is complete, which can lead to late discovery of issues while agile incorporates continuous testing and feedback, ensuring early detection of bugs and faster resolutions.
Waterfall is best suited for well-defined and predictable projects where requirements are clear from the start. For example, when developing a medical records system for a hospital, regulatory compliance and strict documentation are necessary, making the structured Waterfall approach ideal.
Agile is ideal for dynamic projects where requirements may change frequently. For instance, developing a mobile app for an e-commerce platform benefits from Agile’s flexibility, as it allows developers to continuously update features based on user feedback and market trends.

Describe the roles and responsibilities of a Software Developer, a Quality Assurance Engineer, and a Project Manager in a software engineering team.
A software developer is responsible for designing, coding, and implementing software applications. They transform requirements into functional programs using programming languages and frameworks. Their key responsibilities include; Writing clean, efficient, and maintainable code, debugging and troubleshooting software issues, collaborating with designers, testers, and other team members, integrating third-party services and APIs and maintaining and updating software based on user feedback and new requirements.

A Quality Assurance engineer ensures that the software meets quality standards before deployment. They identify bugs, improve test processes, and ensure smooth functionality. Their key responsibilities include; designing and executing test cases for software validation, identifying and documenting software defects and performance issues, conducting manual and automated testing to ensure reliability, working closely with developers to resolve issues and ensuring the software meets security, usability, and performance standards.

A project manager oversees the software development process, ensuring that the project is completed on time, within budget, and meets business goals. Their key responsibilities include; planning and defining project scope, goals, and deliverables, assigning tasks and managing team workflow, monitoring progress and ensuring deadlines are met, communicating with stakeholders and resolving project challenges and managing risks and implementing solutions to keep the project on track.

Discuss the importance of Integrated Development Environments (IDEs) and Version Control Systems (VCS) in the software development process. Give examples of each.
IDEs streamline the software development process by providing a comprehensive workspace that integrates various tools, making coding more efficient and error-free. They offer features like syntax highlighting, code auto-completion, debugging tools, and built-in terminals, helping developers write, test, and manage code effectively. An example is Visual Studio Code (VS Code) – A lightweight, versatile IDE with extensions for various programming languages.

VCS helps teams track, manage, and collaborate on code changes efficiently. It allows multiple developers to work on a project simultaneously, revert to previous versions if needed, and resolve conflicts when merging changes. VCS improves code reliability, collaboration, and recovery from errors. Examples include Git – A distributed VCS widely used for software development, allowing branching and collaboration and GitHub – A cloud-based Git repository hosting service that enables team collaboration and version tracking.

What are some common challenges faced by software engineers? Provide strategies to overcome these challenges.
Identifying and resolving complex bugs can be time-consuming and frustrating.
Solution: Use debugging tools, log analysis, and systematic testing (unit tests, integration tests) to pinpoint issues efficiently. Collaborating with team members and leveraging online developer communities (e.g., Stack Overflow) can also help.
The tech industry evolves rapidly, requiring engineers to constantly learn new programming languages, frameworks, and tools.
Solution: Engage in continuous learning through online courses, workshops, tech blogs, and attending developer conferences. Being active in coding communities also helps.
Tight deadlines and shifting priorities can lead to stress and reduced code quality.
Solution: Use Agile methodologies like Scrum to break projects into manageable sprints. Prioritize tasks using project management tools (e.g., Jira, Trello) and communicate effectively with stakeholders to set realistic expectations.

Explain the different types of testing (unit, integration, system, and acceptance) and their importance in software quality assurance.
Unit Testing; involves testing individual components or functions of the software in isolation. It ensures that each module works correctly before integration.The importance is it detects bugs early, reducing debugging time and improving code reliability.
Integration Testing; examines the interaction between multiple units or components to ensure they work together.It identifies issues in data flow and communication between modules.The importance is it prevents failures caused by mismatched interfaces or dependencies.
System Testing; evaluates the entire software application as a complete system to verify that it meets specified requirements.It tests software behavior in a real-world environment.Its importance is that it ensures overall system functionality, performance, and compliance with business requirements.
Acceptance Testing; it validates whether the software meets end-user expectations and business requirements before deployment to confirms readiness for production and user satisfaction.
It is important that it ensures that the final product is user-friendly, bug-free, and ready for market release.

#Part 2: Introduction to AI and Prompt Engineering


Define prompt engineering and discuss its importance in interacting with AI models.
Prompt engineering is the practice of crafting well-structured inputs (prompts) to effectively communicate with AI models and obtain accurate, relevant, and high-quality responses. 

Importance of Prompt Engineering in AI Interaction
Improves Response Accuracy – Well-designed prompts reduce ambiguity, helping AI generate more precise and relevant answers. 
Enhances AI Efficiency – Effective prompts reduce back-and-forth interactions, saving time and effort when extracting insights or automating tasks.
Optimizes AI for Specific Tasks – Tailored prompts can guide AI to perform specialized functions, such as writing code, summarizing articles, or generating creative content.
Reduces Bias and Misinterpretation – Carefully crafted prompts help minimize misleading or biased responses, ensuring fair and objective outputs from AI models.
